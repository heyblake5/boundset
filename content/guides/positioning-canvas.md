## H1
Positioning canvas template

## DEFINITION (40-60 words)
A positioning canvas is a one page worksheet that captures five positioning choices in order. The boxes are competitive alternatives, unique attributes, value, best fit customers and market category, plus a one line summary. You fill it from real deal notes and customer words, then write your messaging from it.

## KEY TAKEAWAYS
- The canvas has six boxes. Fill them in order, because each box depends on the one before it.
- The five core boxes follow April Dunford's method from Obviously Awesome. The summary line follows the classic positioning statement format often traced to Geoffrey Moore.
- Fill the canvas from evidence such as won and lost deals, sales call recordings and customer quotes.
- Date the canvas and review it when your product or best customers change.
- The canvas is internal. Use it to write messaging. Keep it off your homepage.
- Each box on this page is an interactive exercise. Your answers form your finished canvas.

## How to use this canvas

Work through the boxes from top to bottom. Each box has a prompt and a worked example. The example uses Acme Billing, a fictional invoicing tool for small accounting firms. It is for illustration only.

Before you start, gather three things. Notes from your last 10 to 20 won and lost deals. One or two recorded sales calls. The words your happiest customers use to describe you. Plan for a focused working session with no more than three people. Too many voices slow the work down.

| Box | Question | Where the answer comes from |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Competitive alternatives | What would buyers use if you did not exist? | Won and lost deal notes |
| 2 Unique attributes | What do you have that those alternatives lack? | Product, sales demos, customer interviews |
| 3 Value | What does each attribute let customers do? | Customer quotes |
| 4 Best fit customers | Who cares most about that value, and why? | Fastest closes, longest retained customers |
| 5 Market category | What frame makes the value obvious? | Your answers to boxes 1 to 4 |
| 6 Positioning statement | Can you say it in one line? | All five boxes |

## Box 1 Competitive alternatives

Competitive alternatives are what buyers would use if your product did not exist. Start here because value only makes sense compared with something. Include the status quo. Spreadsheets and doing nothing often show up more than direct competitors.

## Box 2 Unique attributes

Unique attributes are the things you have that the alternatives in box 1 lack. Features count. So do a business model, a data source, a delivery method or a team. Cross out anything an alternative can also do.

[EXERCISE positioning-canvas-ex-2]
**Title:** Unique attributes
**Why it matters:** Buyers decide based on differences. Shared features do not help them choose.
**Prompt:** What do you have that those alternatives lack? List each attribute and which alternative lacks it.
**Worked example (fictional):** Reads client emails and drafts invoices (spreadsheets and the suite lack this). Sends payment reminders on a schedule (spreadsheets lack this). Set up in one afternoon for firms under 20 staff (the suite takes weeks).

## Box 3 Value

Value is what each unique attribute lets a customer do. Customers buy the result, so write it in their words. Add "so you can" after each attribute and finish the sentence until you reach a result someone would pay for.

[EXERCISE positioning-canvas-ex-3]
**Title:** Value
**Why it matters:** This box turns product facts into reasons to buy. Messaging is written mostly from here.
**Prompt:** What does each attribute let customers do? Write one value line per attribute, then one line for the combined value.
**Worked example (fictional):** Drafts invoices from emails so firms bill the same day the work is done. Sends reminders so owners stop chasing late payments by hand. Combined: firms get paid sooner without adding staff.
**Your answer:**
______
______
Combined value: ______
[/EXERCISE]

## Box 4 Best fit customers

Best fit customers are the buyers who care most about the value in box 3. Go past company size and industry. Find the trait that makes the value urgent, such as a task the buyer does every week or a cost they feel every month.

[EXERCISE positioning-canvas-ex-4]
**Title:** Best fit customers
**Why it matters:** A specific target makes every message easier to write and tells sales who to call first.
**Prompt:** Who cares most about that value, and why? Write the traits your best customers share and the one trait that makes it urgent.
**Worked example (fictional):** Accounting firms with 3 to 15 staff that bill hourly and send more than 50 invoices a month. Urgent trait: the owner does billing personally, often at night.
**Your answer:**
Shared traits: ______
Urgent trait: ______
[/EXERCISE]

## Box 5 Market category

Market category is the frame that tells buyers what to compare you with. Pick it last. Write a few candidate categories, note what buyers would assume under each and choose the one where those assumptions help you.

[EXERCISE positioning-canvas-ex-5]
**Title:** Market category
**Why it matters:** The category sets expectations before a buyer reads your copy. The right one makes your value obvious.
**Prompt:** What frame makes the value obvious? List candidate categories, what buyers would assume under each and your pick.
**Worked example (fictional):** "Accounting software": compared to full suites, looks thin. "Invoicing tool": compared to cheap templates. "Automated billing for small accounting firms": compared to doing it by hand. Pick: automated billing for small accounting firms.
**Your answer:**
Candidates and assumptions: ______
Pick: ______
[/EXERCISE]

## Box 6 Positioning statement

The positioning statement is a one line summary of the five boxes. The common template reads: For [customer] who [need], [product] is a [category] that [value]. Unlike [alternative], we [attribute]. Use it to check that the boxes fit together.

April Dunford argues that a positioning statement on its own is a weak way to do positioning, because it assumes there is only one right answer. That is why this canvas puts the statement last. Write it after the five boxes. Treat it as an internal summary and keep it off your homepage.

[EXERCISE positioning-canvas-ex-6]
**Title:** Positioning statement
**Why it matters:** If the sentence reads awkwardly, one of the boxes above probably does not fit with the others.
**Prompt:** Fill in the template using boxes 1 to 5.
**Worked example (fictional):** For small accounting firms whose owners do billing themselves, Acme Billing is automated billing software that turns client emails into sent invoices the same day. Unlike spreadsheets and email templates, Acme Billing drafts invoices and chases late payments for you.
**Your answer:**
For ______ who ______, ______ is a ______ that ______. Unlike ______, we ______.
[/EXERCISE]

## How to check your finished canvas

A finished canvas should read as one connected argument. Read it from box 1 to box 6 and check each link. If a link breaks, fix the box before it.

| Check | Pass looks like |
|---|---|
| Every attribute beats at least one alternative | No shared features left in box 2 |
| Every value line traces to an attribute | No value without a cause |
| Best fit customers care about the value | You can name real customers who match |
| The category makes the value obvious | A best fit buyer would compare you to the alternatives you chose |
| The statement reads naturally | A teammate can say it aloud without stumbling |

Then test it outside the room. Use the new framing on your next few sales calls and ask prospects how they would describe you to a colleague.

## What to do with the canvas next

Use the canvas to write your messaging. The category and combined value become your homepage headline and one line description. The attributes become feature sections. ## FAQ
**Q: What is a positioning canvas?**
A: A positioning canvas is a one page worksheet that records your positioning choices in order. It covers competitive alternatives, unique attributes, value, best fit customers and market category, with a one line positioning statement at the end. Teams fill it from real deal notes and use it as the source for messaging.

**Q: Where does this canvas come from?**
A: The five core boxes follow April Dunford's positioning method from her book Obviously Awesome. The summary line uses the classic positioning statement format often traced to Geoffrey Moore's Crossing the Chasm. Boundset combined them into one page and added prompts and a worked example for each box.

**Q: How long does it take to fill in a positioning canvas?**
A: A first draft can take a few hours if you have deal notes and sales call recordings ready. Getting a team to agree on every box often takes longer. Plan a focused session with a small group, then test the result on real sales calls before you treat it as final.

**Q: Can I put my positioning statement on my homepage?**
A: Use it as an internal check. The statement packs every positioning choice into one sentence, which reads awkwardly to buyers. Homepage copy should be shorter and written in buyer language. Use the category and combined value boxes to write a headline instead.

**Q: How often should I update my positioning canvas?**
A: Update it when your alternatives, best fit customers or product change in a meaningful way. Examples include a new product line or a competitor that changes how buyers compare options. Date the canvas each time you review it so the team knows how current it is.

**Q: What is the difference between a positioning canvas and a value proposition canvas?**
A: A positioning canvas captures company level strategy, including alternatives and market category. A value proposition canvas maps what messages matter to which audience. You usually fill the positioning canvas first, then use it to write value propositions for each persona.
