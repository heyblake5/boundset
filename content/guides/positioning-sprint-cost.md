## What positioning sprints cost

Positioning sprints for startups usually cost a few thousand dollars and run two to four weeks. The prices below come from each provider's own public page, checked in October 2026. Prices change, so confirm on the provider's site before you decide.

| Provider | Published price | Length | What the page says is included |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rethink GTM | $2,400 one time | 3 weeks, plus a 4th week for revisions if needed | One positioning strategy and one core page. Positioning memo, core messaging, homepage copy, Figma wireframes, sales deck wireframes, a 2 pager and a GTM activation plan |
| SF Marketing Agency | $7,500 fixed scope | 14 business days | Positioning, messaging and GTM sequencing. The page says it does not include a logo or brand book |
| Boundset Stake | $7,500 | 2 weeks | Positioning workshop, strategy grid, messaging matrix, use case messaging, homepage copy with variants, wireframe, competitive audit, one activation asset, ongoing Slack access |
| Boundset Stronghold | $12,000 | 2 weeks plus ongoing coaching | Everything in Stake plus three more pages, three activation assets and a year of async coaching in Slack |
| April Dunford workshop | $50,000 (10 to 50 people) or $80,000 (50 to 1,000+ people) | 3 day facilitated workshop | Her five component positioning plus sales pitch development. The larger tier adds advisory sessions |

April Dunford's workshop sits in its own tier. It is a facilitated session for an executive team run by the author of Obviously Awesome. Compare it to sprints on what your team needs. Price alone will mislead you. The April Dunford alternatives guide covers that choice in more depth.

## What drives the price of a positioning sprint

The price of a positioning sprint depends mostly on scope. Lower priced sprints usually cover one positioning strategy and one page. Higher priced sprints add more pages and assets or support after the sprint ends.

### Deliverables
Some sprints end with a strategy document. Others include finished homepage copy your team can use the next day. Count the deliverables and ask for a sample of each format.

### Pages and assets
A homepage is the usual core page. Product, feature, use case and comparison pages cost more. So do sales assets such as cold email sequences, objection scripts or investor messaging.

### Who does the work
At some firms a senior person sells the project and a junior team does the work. At others one person does the research and the writing. Ask who will be in the sessions and who will write the copy.

### Support after the sprint
Some providers end on the last day. Others include Slack access or ongoing coaching. Support matters because questions come up as your team starts using the new messaging.

### Your team's time
The sticker price leaves out your hours. A sprint that needs three full days of executive time costs more in practice than one that needs a few hours. Ask for a clear estimate.

## What Boundset costs and what you get

Boundset costs $7,500 for the Stake sprint or $12,000 for Stronghold. Both run as a two week sprint for seed stage startups and beyond. Stake covers your core messaging and homepage. Stronghold adds more pages and assets plus a year of coaching.

### Stake
Stake costs $7,500.

- Positioning workshop, a 90 minute working session
- Positioning strategy grid with your positioning statement, problem framing, value proposition and supporting arguments
- Messaging matrix
- Use case messaging doc
- Minimum viable messaging doc
- Value proposition canvas
- Homepage copy with variants for every heading
- Homepage wireframe with the copy in place
- Brand vs founder guide
- Competitive positioning audit
- One activation asset you pick
- Ongoing Slack access with no retainer

Activation asset options: cold email sequence of 5 to 7 emails, welcome sequence, landing page copy, objection scripts, 30 days of hooks, ad copy kit, investor messaging or a case study framework.

### Stronghold
Stronghold costs $12,000.

- Everything in Stake
- Three additional pages written, such as product, feature or use case pages
- Three activation assets
- One year of async coaching in Slack

### Payment and terms
Payment is 50% to start and 50% on delivery. If you start with Stake and upgrade to Stronghold within 30 days, what you paid for Stake counts toward Stronghold. The price is a flat fee. One formal revision round is included, and smaller tweaks happen in Slack. Past clients get priority and a reduced rate for refresh projects.

### Your time
The sprint takes about four to five hours of your time over two weeks. That covers one 90 minute workshop, a few review sessions and async feedback. Gathering materials before the start takes about 30 minutes. You send a product demo, a recorded sales call, your current site and a competitor list.

## How to compare quotes

Compare quotes by putting them side by side on the same list of deliverables and terms. A cheaper sprint that leaves you to write the copy yourself can cost more once you add a copywriter. A pricier one may include work you would buy anyway.

| Question to ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What exact files do I get at the end? | Separates strategy only sprints from ones that include finished copy |
| How many pages are written? | Pages beyond the homepage often cost extra |
| Who runs the workshop and who writes? | Tells you whether the senior person does the work |
| How many hours does my team need? | Your time is part of the cost |
| How many revision rounds are included? | Avoids surprise fees at the end |
| What happens after the last day? | Support matters when your team starts using the work |
| What are the payment terms? | Affects cash flow for an early stage company |

## How to judge whether a sprint is worth it

A sprint is worth it when the cost of unclear positioning is higher than the price. That cost shows up as long sales calls, deals lost to confusion and repeated homepage rewrites. Put rough numbers on what you spend on those today.

Things to add up:

- Hours founders and sales spend explaining the product on calls
- Money spent on past homepage rewrites or copywriters
- Deals where the prospect compared you to the wrong product
- Time new hires take to describe the company well
- Spend planned for outbound or a site rebuild that will run on the current message

## When a sprint is the wrong buy

A sprint is the wrong buy when you are still deciding what to build. Positioning works best once you have a product and customers who show you who cares most. It is also the wrong buy if you need months of launches and sales enablement. A fractional product marketer may fit better there.
