## H1
Positioning agency vs fractional PMM vs positioning sprint

## DEFINITION (40-60 words)
A positioning agency pairs strategy with design and production for a full brand or site project. A fractional product marketing manager (PMM) joins your team part time for months to run positioning and launches. A positioning sprint sets positioning in a fixed window for a fixed price. The right choice depends on stage and scope.

## KEY TAKEAWAYS
- All three are good options. Each fits a different stage and a different problem.
- Pick an agency when you need strategy plus a new brand and site built at once.
- Pick a fractional PMM when you need ongoing product marketing work such as launches and sales enablement over several months.
- Pick a sprint when the core story is unclear and you need it settled fast with a fixed scope and price.
- Many teams combine them. Settle positioning first, then hand an agency or a PMM a clear brief.
- Ask every option the same questions about deliverables, who does the work and what happens when the engagement ends.

## The short answer

A positioning agency fits a team that needs brand design and copy delivered together. A fractional PMM fits a team that needs a part time product marketer for ongoing work. A positioning sprint fits a team whose story is unclear and who needs positioning and copy settled in weeks.

| | Positioning or messaging agency | Fractional PMM | Positioning sprint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best for | A full brand and site project | Ongoing product marketing work | Settling positioning and core copy fast |
| Engagement | Project, often with design and build | Part time role over months | Fixed scope with an end date |
| Pricing model | Project fee or retainer, varies by agency | Monthly fee or hourly, varies | Flat fee |
| Who does the work | A team with strategy and design roles | One senior marketer inside your team | Usually one strategist or a small team |
| Typical output | Brand identity, site, messaging | Launches, sales enablement, positioning updates | Positioning doc, messaging, core page copy |
| Your involvement | Reviews across the project | Regular meetings, like a team member | A few focused sessions |
| Watch for | Scope and timeline growing | Strategy waiting behind daily tasks | Work after the sprint needs an owner |

Pricing varies widely by provider in all three groups, so get quotes on the same scope. The positioning sprint cost guide lists published sprint prices.

## When a positioning agency is the right choice

A positioning or messaging agency is the right choice when you need strategy and execution in one project. Agencies bring design and copy under one roof, and often web development too. Teams usually pick one for a rebrand, a new site or a launch that needs a full visual identity.

Strong agency fits:

- You are rebranding and need a new visual identity
- You need a full site designed and built, not only written
- You want one partner to manage the whole project
- You have the budget and time for a longer project

What to ask an agency:

- Who will lead the strategy work, and will they be in every session?
- Is positioning a separate phase with its own sign off?
- What happens if positioning changes the scope of the design work?

## When a fractional PMM is the right choice

A fractional PMM is the right choice when product marketing is ongoing work and you are not ready for a full time hire. A fractional PMM works inside your team part time for months. They can refine positioning, run launches, build sales materials and train reps.

Strong fractional PMM fits:

- You ship often and need launch support every month
- Sales needs ongoing enablement such as battlecards and talk tracks
- You plan to hire a full time PMM later and want the role shaped first
- Your positioning is mostly settled and needs upkeep

What to ask a fractional PMM:

- How many hours a week will you give us, and how many clients do you have?
- How much of the first month goes to positioning versus execution?
- What will you hand off if we hire full time?

## When a positioning sprint is the right choice

A positioning sprint is the right choice when your story is unclear and you need it fixed quickly. A sprint has a fixed scope and an end date. Some sprints stop at strategy. Others include messaging and finished copy your team can use right away.

Strong sprint fits:

- Your team describes the company in different ways
- Prospects compare you to the wrong products
- You keep rewriting the homepage and it does not get clearer
- You are about to hire an agency or PMM and want to give them a clear brief
- You have a working product and paying customers

What to ask a sprint provider:

- What exact files do I get at the end?
- Does the sprint include copy, or only strategy?
- What support do I get after the last day?

## How to choose between them

To choose, match the option to your main problem and your stage. If the story itself is unclear, start with positioning. If the story is clear and you need it delivered as a brand or site, an agency fits. If you need someone running product marketing every week, a fractional PMM fits.

| If your main problem is | Start with |
|---|---|
| The team cannot agree on what we are or who we serve | Positioning sprint |
| The story is clear but the brand looks dated | Agency |
| We need a full site designed and built | Agency, with positioning settled first |
| We launch often and need ongoing support | Fractional PMM |
| Sales needs battlecards and training every quarter | Fractional PMM |
| We need positioning and homepage copy in weeks | Positioning sprint |

## Why many teams do positioning first

Many teams settle positioning before hiring an agency or a PMM because both work better with a clear brief. An agency can design faster when the message is decided. A fractional PMM can start on launches in week one instead of rebuilding the story.

The order also protects your budget. Design and production are the expensive parts of an agency project. If positioning changes halfway through, that work often gets redone.

## FAQ
**Q: Should a seed stage startup hire a fractional PMM?**
A: Hire a fractional PMM when you need ongoing product marketing work such as monthly launches and sales enablement, and you are not ready for a full time hire. If your core story still shifts from week to week, settle positioning first with a sprint so the PMM can start on execution right away.

**Q: What is the difference between a positioning agency and a fractional PMM?**
A: An agency runs a project with a team that often includes strategists and designers. It fits rebrands and site builds. A fractional PMM is one senior marketer who works inside your team part time for months. It fits ongoing work such as launches and sales materials.

**Q: Is a positioning sprint enough on its own?**
A: For many early teams, yes. A sprint that includes messaging and copy gives you positioning plus pages you can publish. You still need someone to use the work over time. That can be the founder, a marketing hire, a fractional PMM or an agency working from the new brief.

**Q: Do I need positioning before hiring an agency?**
A: It helps. Agencies design and build faster with a settled message, and design work is often the most expensive part of the project. If positioning changes halfway through, finished design may need to be redone. Many teams settle positioning first, then give the agency a written brief.

**Q: Does Boundset design websites?**
A: No. Boundset writes the copy and delivers a homepage wireframe with sections laid out and the copy in place. Your designer handles the visual design. Boundset is happy to answer questions from your designer or developer so the work ships as intended.

**Q: How do I compare quotes across these options?**
A: Put every quote against the same list. Ask what files you get, who does the work and how many hours your team needs. Ask what happens when the engagement ends. Pricing models differ, so convert each to a total for the scope you need before you compare.
